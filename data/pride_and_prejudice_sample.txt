Elizabeth and Darcy walked through the garden together.

Mrs. Bennet and Kitty were discussing the upcoming ball in the parlor. Lydia burst into the room and shouted the news about the regiment to them.

Elizabeth and Jane were conversing in the drawing room, while Mr. Darcy and Caroline Bingley were walking in the garden. Mr. Bingley entered the drawing room and asked the sisters if they would like tea.

Wickham's arrival caused a stir, and Darcy's disapproval was evident to Elizabeth.

When Mrs. Bennet introduced her daughters to Mr. Bingley, Elizabeth observed his reaction carefully.

Both Elizabeth and Darcy realized their feelings for one another during their time at Pemberley.

At the Netherfield ball, Elizabeth danced with Darcy while Jane spoke with Bingley. Mrs. Bennet watched them all proudly, and Mr. Darcy's sister Georgiana observed from the side. 

Caroline Bingley felt increasing jealousy toward Elizabeth as she saw her with Mr. Darcy.

The door opened and Miss Bennet entered, followed closely by her sister Elizabeth. Mr. Bingley immediately rose to greet the former, while Mr. Darcy merely glanced at the latter with an air of cold indifference.

"I must assure you, Mrs. Bennet," said Mr. Collins pompously, "that Lady Catherine de Bourgh has been most kind. She often invites me to Rosings for tea."

Elizabeth remained seated in the corner, obscured by a large fern. She heard Mr. Darcy say to Bingley, "She is tolerable, but not handsome enough to tempt me."

Mr. Wickham had charmed the entire room with his easy manners and ready smile. He moved gracefully through the crowd, accepting compliments from the ladies. Finally, he turned to Elizabeth and bowed low.

Lydia and Kitty ran up to the officers, laughing loudly, and demanded they all buy them ribbons. Colonel Forster and Captain Carter laughed in return and promised to do so.

Elizabeth's hands trembled as she broke the seal. "Be not alarmed, Madam, on receiving this letter..." wrote Darcy. She read the pages with a mixture of pain and astonishment.

Elizabeth walked up to Mr. Darcy and asked if he liked the dance. He did not answer, but turned his heel and walked away in silence.

Mr. Bingley walked into the room, and his wife immediately began to chide him for his lateness.

"If I may observe—" began Mary, but she was cut off by Lydia who shouted across the table to her mother.

Mr. Collins handed a flower to Charlotte, though his eyes were fixed apologetically on Mrs. Bennet.

Mr. Collins, Mr. Phillips and Mr. Bingley enterd the room. Mr. Bingley went straight to Jane, but the other stood aloof near the fireplace, staring at the floor.

The children were immediately swept up by their cousins, while Mrs. Gardiner went quietly to sit with Elizabeth.

Elizabeth played a lively Scottish air. Mr. Darcy drew near the piano to listen, while Colonel Fitzwilliam asked her to play it faster.

Lady Catherine de Bourgh walked alongside Elizabeth in the garden, speaking incessantly, while Mr. Collins trailed three paces behind them, too terrified to speak.
